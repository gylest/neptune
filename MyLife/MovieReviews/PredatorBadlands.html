Review: Predator: Badlands

A bold perspective shift turns the galaxy's deadliest hunter into an unlikely sci-fi underdog.

Posted on: Nov 1, 2026 • By Tony Gyles

Starring Dimitrius Schuster-Koloamatangi, Elle Fanning
Rating PG-13 / 12A
Key Themes Yautja Lore, Survival, Weyland-Yutani Crossover

A Brutal Opening and a Bold Perspective Shift

The harshness of the Predator universe is established within the very first fifteen minutes of Predator: Badlands, plunging viewers straight into intense, unforgiving encounters between two brothers and their father. Rather than easing the audience into this world, the opening act immediately sets the stakes of a brutal warrior culture where weakness is never tolerated.

From there, the film makes its boldest creative gamble by completely flipping the franchise's traditional perspective. Instead of positioning the alien as a shadowy, unseen stalker hunting human soldiers through the jungle, Badlands centers its story on a young Yautja—an outcast "runt" named Dek (played with remarkable physical expressiveness by Dimitrius Schuster-Koloamatangi)—making the alien hunter the film's central underdog protagonist.

Deep Yautja World-Building & A Hostile Alien Ecology

Long-time fans of the lore will find plenty to admire in the film's cultural world-building. Badlands dives deep into Yautja society, officially canonising species terminology on screen and introducing a distinct, spoken alien language accompanied by subtitles rather than relying on awkward human exposition.

Structurally, the movie departs from the claustrophobic survival horror of the 1987 original and 2022's Prey, shifting gears toward a sweeping sci-fi action-adventure quest reminiscent of space operas and survival video games. Much of this quest unfolds across the treacherous surface of planet Genna, where the environment itself serves as a relentless antagonist. Inventive, lethal alien flora and fauna lurk around every corner, forcing Dek to constantly adapt his hunting tactics just to stay alive.

Navigating the PG-13 / 12A Rating

As the first mainline theatrical entry in the franchise to carry a PG-13 (12A) rating, some fans worried the action might be watered down. Thankfully, the film finds an effective workaround: it retains visceral action and gruesome dismemberment because the carnage primarily involves synthetic white fluid and glowing alien slime rather than red human blood.

An Unlikely Odd-Couple & A Seamless Alien Crossover

At the emotional core of the movie is an unlikely "buddy comedy" dynamic between Dek and Thia, a damaged, limbless Weyland-Yutani synthetic android played by Elle Fanning. Strapped to the young Predator's back as they traverse the badlands, the pair share a genuinely humorous and surprisingly heartfelt odd-couple chemistry that gives the relentless action room to breathe.

Fanning delivers a standout dual performance, showcasing impressive charisma and physical acting—both in bringing much-needed levity as the carried companion Thia and in portraying her cold, calculating corporate antagonist android counterpart. Better still, the integration of Weyland-Yutani elements never feels like a cheap studio callback. Instead, the crossover with the Alien universe feels grounded, organic to the plot, and sets up a compelling broader continuity for future entries.

Visual Craft vs. The "Disneyfication" Debate

Visually, Predator: Badlands makes great use of rugged New Zealand location photography blended with practical creature craft. That said, the visual effects aren't completely immune to modern blockbuster pitfalls—certain digital creatures and the darker opening sequences occasionally feel overly reliant on glossy, video game-style CGI.

The film has also sparked a lively debate among franchise purists online. While many embrace the fresh direction, some traditionalists argue that giving the Predator a sympathetic underdog arc, emotional vulnerabilities, and "found family" story beats softens the creature's ruthless, terrifying mystique. Whether this tonal evolution works for you will largely depend on whether you want the franchise to repeat its 1987 formula or chart new territory.

✔ What Works

  • Bold perspective shift with Dek as a compelling Yautja underdog.
  • Fantastic chemistry between Schuster-Koloamatangi and Elle Fanning.
  • Rich Yautja language, lore, and inventive predators on planet Genna.
  • Organic, meaningful Weyland-Yutani tie-in with the Alien universe.
  • Clever use of synthetic and alien gore keeps the PG-13 action fierce.

⚠ Where It Falters

  • Some darker early scenes and digital beasts lean heavily on video-game CGI.
  • Hardcore horror purists may miss the terrifying, enigmatic mystique of the original hunter.

Final Verdict

Rating: 4 / 5 Stars

Predator: Badlands trades claustrophobic slasher tension for an ambitious, world-expanding sci-fi adventure. Even if a few CGI-heavy moments show their seams and its warmer character beats divide die-hard purists, the gripping opening, deadly planetary ecology, and standout odd-couple pairing of Dek and Thia make this one of the most entertaining and inventive entries in the franchise.
